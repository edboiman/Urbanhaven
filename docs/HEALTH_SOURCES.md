# Water, food, sanitation, and first aid content review

Checked against the following primary-source pages on **2026-10-31**. This is an editorial source check, not independent clinical review or an endorsement by a source organization. Articles are original plain-English summaries, not republished pages or PDFs. The application links to the originals for attribution; it does not download those sites at runtime.

## Safety decisions

- Water planning uses CDC's **at least 1 US gallon per person per day**, a 3-day starting supply, and a 2-week goal if possible. Pet and extra needs are added separately. The result is a planning estimate, not a survival guarantee or a prescription to ration fluid.
- Self-filled water rotates every 6 months. Commercial bottled-water dates follow the manufacturer. Food-grade containers must never have held toxic chemicals.
- The container-sanitizing recipe is explicitly different from drinking-water disinfection: 1 teaspoon of unscented 5–9% sodium hypochlorite bleach in 1 quart of water, coat the interior, wait at least 30 seconds, empty, air-dry, then fill safely.
- Boiling: full rolling boil for 1 minute, or 3 minutes above 6,500 feet. Boiling and disinfection do not make fuel/chemically/radioactively contaminated water safe.
- Emergency bleach treatment is limited to **regular unscented 5–9% sodium hypochlorite**. Follow drinking-water label directions where available. Otherwise CDC specifies 8 drops per US gallon of clear water; double for cloudy, murky, colored, or very cold water. Prefilter/settle cloudiness, stir, and wait at least 30 minutes. Chlorine is less effective against some parasites. Unknown concentrations, scented products, and additive-containing products are excluded.
- Filtration is described by organism-specific claims and manufacturer limitations. A simple membrane or cloth is never called universal purification.
- Boil, do-not-drink, and do-not-use advisories are distinguished; local instructions control allowed uses and clearance.
- Refrigerator timing is about 4 hours with its door closed; a closed full freezer about 48 hours and half-full about 24. Actual temperature and food-specific guidance matter. No taste tests. Thawed foods with ice crystals or at 40°F or below may generally be refrozen. Perishable food above 40°F for over 2 hours generally needs disposal.
- Low-acid canning requires a researched pressure-canning process. No invented process or emergency salvage recipe is supplied. No assumption that an electric multicooker is an approved pressure canner.
- Camp stoves, grills, and similar outdoor combustion equipment remain outdoors, away from openings and as required by their manuals. Indoor use, garage use, and space heating are explicitly prohibited.
- Sewer failure is distinguished from loss of tap pressure. Emergency toilet waste is contained in double heavy-duty bags, protected from exposure, and disposed of only under local rules; no general permission for burial or ordinary trash is given.
- Surface products follow label contact time, dilution, and food-contact rinse requirements. No universal surface-bleach concentration is reused for drinking water. Never mix cleaners.
- First aid stays within basic initial measures and escalation. No medication doses, antibiotic recommendations, complex untrained procedures, or promises of diagnosis. Severe bleeding is pressure plus emergency help; tourniquets/packing require training. Burns use Red Cross's current 5–20-minute cool-water instruction and urgent-care criteria. Suspected sprains are treated as possible fractures.
- Medicine storage is product-specific; no blanket insulin or refrigerated-drug time limit. The FDA's lifesaving-drug considerations require urgent professional advice rather than abruptly stopping essential treatment.
- The guide never claims an emergency call or medical response will work without infrastructure.

## Scope and review limitations

This first release contains 30 guides in these four categories (8 water, 8 food, 6 sanitation, 8 medical). Some broad requested topics are covered only within related guides rather than individual articles. These are US-focused educational references. Local advisories, product instructions, clinicians, and public-health authorities take precedence where appropriate. Source availability and content may change after the check date; maintaining the app requires periodic editorial re-verification. No third-party source logos, full PDFs, commercial illustrations, or site text are bundled by this content pack.
