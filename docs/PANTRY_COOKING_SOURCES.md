# Pantry reference and outdoor cooking sources

Checked: **2026-10-31 (America/Detroit)**.

This addition contains 15 pantry groups (9 Core and 6 Useful extra) and 10 original pantry recipes. Core identifies practical food roles—carbohydrate energy, protein, fat, produce and calcium foods—not a claim that every household must buy every listed example. Portions, allergies, cultural preferences, infant feeding and prescribed diets still need household planning. These entries supplement the application's separate safe-water guidance.

## Editorial limits

- The recipes are original combinations, not USDA, CDC or Forest Service recipes or endorsements. Ingredient quantities, serving counts, water ranges and elapsed times are practical planning estimates; they have not been kitchen-tested. Package instructions and actual cooking endpoints take priority over the estimate. Each `sourceUrl` supports the relevant safety context, not every recipe quantity.
- Only commercial foods labeled shelf-stable and, where specified, fully cooked/ready-to-eat are assumed. A refrigerated ham, refrigerated milk carton, raw poultry or raw dry bean is not an interchangeable ingredient. No home-canned food is assumed.
- Pantry `unopenedLife` describes best-quality rotation, separately from safe handling. Numeric examples are tied to individual foods and preparation/packaging states, including canned foods, grains, spreads, oils, UHT milk, dry beans, nuts, dried fruit and seasonings. These are approximate quality planning windows, not universal safety expirations. Product labels, package condition and storage still matter; no multi-year rule is applied across an entire mixed group.
- U.S. best-quality dates do not certify safety. A damaged package, recall or unsafe temperature history overrides its date. Follow safety-related storage/use instructions and infant-formula use-by dates. Do not taste suspect food to decide whether it is safe.
- Wet opened foods and cooked meals cannot be assumed shelf-stable. Refrigerated canned-food durations are deliberately not presented as room-temperature permissions. Single-meal cans/cartons reduce leftover handling. The repeated recipe discard limit is for meals without refrigeration; it is not an instruction to wait before eating or chilling.
- All 10 recipes use one cooking vessel; vegetarian tags are explicit. Recipes containing milk or fish are labeled accordingly. Suitable substitutions still require checking labels and preparation directions.

## Water, fuel and outdoor operation

Recipe water figures count added cooking water only. Packing liquids and full single-serving milk cartons are identified separately. Drinking, handwashing, safe utensil cleaning and extinguishing a campfire need additional supplies. No fixed dishwashing allowance is invented. Practice the meal with the actual product and equipment to establish a household reserve; use ready-to-eat food if hygienic preparation cannot be maintained. Use potable water, following local drinking-water advisories; heat does not make chemically contaminated water suitable.

Fuel notes describe the heating task rather than asserting a universal canister life or grams per meal. Stove design, permitted fuel, wind, temperature, altitude, cookware and starting liquid temperature affect consumption. Timings exclude building a campfire, cleaning and safe extinguishment. Follow the stove manufacturer's instructions, including clearances and permitted accessories; do not enclose a fuel canister with an improvised windscreen.

Camp stoves are for open outdoor use. Keep them out of buildings, garages, tents and vehicles because combustion produces carbon monoxide. Opening a door or window does not turn an indoor area into an appropriate cooking site. Check current local fire restrictions before every use; a stove exemption is not universal. Use a stable clear site and stable cookware. Campfire options mean an authorized fire site with a suitable grate over a small controlled fire/coals, attended throughout. Have sufficient extinguishing water ready; without it, do not light a campfire. Drown and stir until completely cold before leaving.

Every recipe empties cans/pouches into suitable cookware and prohibits heating a sealed container. This is an intentional simple equipment rule; the recipes do not teach heating in the can. Heating cannot rescue food that has already been stored unsafely. Where safely chilled leftovers are reheated, use a food thermometer and reach 165°F/74°C; reheated soups should boil. The chicken recipe also identifies the separate 165°F/74°C requirement for raw poultry so that readers do not mistake a canned-chicken warm-up for raw-poultry cooking.
