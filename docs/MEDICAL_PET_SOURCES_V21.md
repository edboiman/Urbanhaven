## Editorial decisions

- Emergency signs lead to escalation even when normal services are disrupted. Delayed access is not treated as permission for invasive care.
- No antibiotic prescriptions, wound closure, drainage, extraction, debridement, or other invasive procedures are included. No homemade oral-rehydration recipe or individualized drug dose is supplied.
- Commercial oral rehydration products require their exact labeled safe-water volume. Infants and people with fluid restrictions need early individual advice.
- NHS clinical principles are adapted to the US emergency number; regional fields make that adaptation visible. UK service numbers are not copied into US action steps.
- Burn cooling follows NHS 20 minutes; Red Cross currently gives a 5–20 minute range. Deep, extensive, chemical, electrical, airway, circumferential, and vulnerable-person burns are escalated.
- CO evacuation and generator placement are distinguished from particle filtration. Conventional pulse oximeters cannot exclude CO poisoning.
- FDA product-specific storage advice replaces universal insulin or medicine storage limits. Lifesaving-drug exceptions are referred for urgent professional advice; visibly altered or wet medicines are excluded.
- Pet food, water, and medicines use CDC’s two-week planning recommendation. AVMA’s general web checklist uses shorter food/water quantities; its Saving the Whole Family guide also has two-week food/water guidance. This release uses 14 days and individualized quantities.
- Cats, dogs, small mammals, birds, and habitat-dependent animals have distinct transport and housing notes. No species-wide fluid, temperature, medication, or food dose is invented.
- Cold-weather pet guidance is additionally grounded in Cornell veterinary material. Safe relocation and veterinary care take priority over improvised heat or medication.

## Retrieval limits and corroboration

- `https://www.ready.gov/pets` returned HTTP 403 to direct retrieval. The canonical link is retained for readers. Ready.gov text was visible in an indexed mirror, but the mirror is not used as authority. Official FEMA Prepare Your Pets for Disasters material and CDC/AVMA independently support the applicable plan and kit advice.
- The direct FEMA Prepare Your Pets for Disasters PDF also returned 403, but its contents were available in indexed official-domain search results. The official Including Your Pets in Disaster PDF was retrieved as indexed primary-source text.
- Current `www.avma.org/resources-tools/…` pages sometimes returned an iframe-only shell. The indexed AVMA canonical legacy page and the directly retrievable AVMA Saving the Whole Family PDF supplied guidance. Warm weather material was retrieved from AVMA’s indexed official page.
- AVMA PDF printed pages 8, 14–15, and 20–21 support crate space, kit planning, and species-specific evacuation respectively. Veterinary medication examples and specialized procedures from that longer document were excluded.
