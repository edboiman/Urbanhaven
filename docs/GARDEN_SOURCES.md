## Crop numbers

- Container depth examples use [NC State Extension: Plants Grown in Containers, Table 18-1](https://content.ces.ncsu.edu/extension-gardener-handbook/18-plants-grown-in-containers): beans 8–10 inches, carrots at least 10 inches, kale 8 inches, lettuce 6–8 inches, peas 12 inches, peppers 12–16 inches, tomatoes 12–24 inches. Radish uses the chapter's conservative 6–8-inch discussion for shallow-rooted crops rather than its smaller table minimum. The chapter also supports 10–12 inches for carrots. Depth alone does not determine an adequate pot: total volume and the cultivar matter.
- Potato and basil depths are deliberately qualitative. No single precise depth is represented as sufficient for every plant or potato container. [Illinois container potatoes](https://extension.illinois.edu/blogs/hort-home-landscape/2015-08-06-growing-potatoes-containers) describes a large container, drainage and adding mix; more recent [Maryland potato guidance](https://www.extension.umd.edu/resource/growing-potatoes-home-garden) cautions that potato towers generally do not work. Do not infer unlimited yield from piling the container higher.
- Approximate days: [UMN fall maturity table](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/planting-vegetables-in-midsummer-for-fall-harvest) supports basil 30–60, bush beans 45–65, kale 40–65, lettuce 40–60, radish 30–60 and fall peas 70–80 days. [MSU fall crops](https://msu-prod.dotcmscloud.com/resources/michigan_fresh_fall_vegetable_crops_for_your_garden) gives example carrot maturity of 60 days and sugar peas of 60 days. A `[60,60]` carrot range represents that source's example, not precision for every cultivar.
- [Maryland potatoes](https://www.extension.umd.edu/resource/growing-potatoes-home-garden) supports 90–120 days from planting; [Maryland tomatoes](https://www.extension.umd.edu/resource/growing-tomatoes-home-garden) supports 65–90 days **from transplant**; [Maryland peppers](https://www.extension.umd.edu/resource/growing-peppers-home-garden) supports 70–85 days **from transplant** for many common types. Colored ripe peppers and some varieties need longer. For other profiles, the displayed interval begins at sowing/planting and can vary by variety and growing conditions. Always prefer the actual seed packet.
- Light and qualitative care use the corresponding crop references plus container guidance. For potatoes, [Iowa State growing potatoes](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/growing-potatoes-home-garden) supports at least six hours of sun. Yield is intentionally not estimated from a container's depth or area. A small garden should not be counted as available emergency food before harvest.

## Frost calculations are planning aids

`springOffsetWeeks` is relative to a user-entered local last-frost date, using MSU's transplant-production table as a planning example: potato -6, pea -7, carrot -2, salad lettuce -3, cooking greens/kale -3, beans +1, basil +1, tomatoes +2, peppers +2 weeks. Radish -2 is a conservative app planning estimate for a cool-season direct-sown root; it is not a universal quoted MSU date. Check the actual weather, soil temperature and packet before sowing. Earliest workable soil and frost tolerance can differ substantially between cultivars and microclimates.

`seedStartWeeks` means approximate weeks **before the outdoor planting date** for transplanted crops, not before last frost: tomato 7, pepper 8, basil 4, kale 4. Zero means this starter profile recommends direct sowing/planting. Seed packet and local extension recommendations override these examples.

For crops with a fall window, `fallWeeksBeforeFrost = ceil((maximum harvestDays + 14)/7)`. The added 14 days is a transparent **application planning margin**, not an extension guarantee or a fixed statewide date. Crop physiology, declining light, actual weather and cold tolerance can require more time. Warm long-season crops are left without a suggested second-sowing date. First frost does not instantly end every hardy crop, nor does hardiness guarantee growth in winter.

Michigan / Great Lakes is the initial regional profile. Other regions require locally appropriate dates, weather and crop advice; this pack does not claim region-specific calendars for every climate. No GPS or location service is necessary.

## Review limits

No crop yield, calorie availability, balcony capacity, irrigation purity or container material safety is guaranteed. Inspect actual product labels, investigate urban soil contamination, follow safe electrical setup around water, and use qualified building advice where structural load is uncertain. This pack has source checking but has not had an independent horticultural or structural review.
