# Storage reference — reviewed 2026-11-01

Use these as planning references, not safety guarantees. Food figures assume intact suitable commercial packaging and cool, dry storage; the exact product label and storage history take priority. Opened food follows a different rule. Seed estimates concern planting viability under ideal conditions, not edible life.

## Water and treatment supplies

| Item | Date rule | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Self-filled drinking water | Replace every 6 calendar months | Sanitized food-grade container, labeled fill date, cool/shaded storage away from chemicals |
| Unopened commercial bottled water | Follow its manufacturer date | No universal number of years is assigned; no self-filled six-month rule |
| Opened or emergency-treated water | No universal duration certified here | Clean covered containers, clean handling and current advisory instructions |
| Emergency drinking-water bleach | Fresh, appropriate liquid bleach; room-temperature storage under one year, with shorter label limits honored | Age and heat reduce strength; unknown age/concentration is unsuitable for guessing doses |

Sources: CDC water-supply guidance, EPA emergency disinfection, FDA flood guidance, linked in SOURCE_AUDIT.md. Flood exposure and damaged closures override any date.

## Pantry food: approximate unopened best-quality examples

| Group | Unopened quality window / example | After opening |
|---|---|---|
| Ready-to-eat grains and crackers | Ready-to-eat cereal: about 6–12 months for best quality in a cool, dry pantry. Crackers and bars follow their own package dates; this is not a safety expiration. | Dry cereal: about 2–3 months for quality when resealed; other products follow the label. Once mixed with milk or other wet ingredients, the mixture becomes perishable. |
| Quick-cooking dry starches | Approximate pantry quality examples: dry pasta 24 months; plain white rice 24 months; brown rice 12 months; oatmeal 6–12 months. Instant grains, blends and pouches follow their specific labels; these are not safety deadlines. | Keep the dry food sealed and dry. Cooked rice, pasta, oats and other moist starches require prompt chilling; without refrigeration, make only the current meal. |
| Canned beans, peas and lentils | Commercial low-acid cans: about 2–5 years for best quality under suitable storage. This is a rotation guide, not a safety guarantee or a required wait. | Refrigerate unused beans promptly. Without refrigeration, use a meal-sized can and discard unused contents after 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F/32°C; never save overnight. |
| Shelf-stable canned fish, poultry or meat | Low-acid commercial cans: about 2–5 years for best quality. Pouches and special products follow their own label; not every canned meat is shelf-stable. | Opened meat and fish are perishable. Refrigerate promptly; without refrigeration, discard unused contents after 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F/32°C. Choose a meal-sized container. |
| Shelf-stable nut or seed butters | Commercial peanut butter: about 6–9 months for best quality in a cool pantry. Other nut/seed butters and natural formulations follow their own labels; this is not a safety expiration. | Typical shelf-stable peanut butter: about 2–3 months for quality. Some natural spreads require refrigeration after opening; follow the label or choose single portions for a no-fridge plan. |
| Cooking oils | Vegetable or olive oil: about 6–12 months for best quality in a cool, dark pantry. Packaging and oil type vary; follow the product date rather than treating this as a safety expiration. | About 3–5 months for quality for typical vegetable/olive oil stored suitably; recap and follow the label. Keep water and food debris out and rotate a small bottle through normal cooking. |
| Canned vegetables and tomatoes | Low-acid vegetable cans: about 2–5 years for best quality; high-acid tomatoes: about 12–18 months. Check the actual product label. | Refrigerate unused contents. Without cold storage, use a meal-sized can and discard unused opened food after 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F/32°C; do not keep it overnight. |
| Canned fruit and unsweetened fruit cups | High-acid commercial fruit cans: about 12–18 months for best quality. Plastic cups, pouches and applesauce follow their own label. | Follow the product's refrigeration instructions after opening. Individual cups avoid planning for an opened container when no refrigerator is available. |
| Shelf-stable milk or fortified soy beverage | Aseptically packaged shelf-stable UHT milk: roughly 6 months as a planning example; exact product dates govern. Fortified soy beverages and milk powder follow their own labels. Refrigerated ultra-pasteurized cartons are not automatically shelf-stable. | Opened milk/soy beverage and reconstituted milk require refrigeration. Without it, use a single portion now and discard after 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F/32°C. Dry powder follows its label. |
| Dry beans, lentils and split peas | Dry beans: about 12–24 months for best quality in ordinary pantry storage. Lentils and split peas follow their labels. Older pulses may soften slowly; this is not a safety expiration. | Keep dry pulses sealed against moisture and pests. Cooked pulses are perishable and require chilling if not eaten promptly. |
| Nuts and seeds | Commercial unopened packaged nut meats: up to about 12 months as a quality example; pine nuts can be much shorter, about 2–3 weeks in the pantry. Exact packaging and label govern; do not generalize to every nut or seed. | Reseal and follow the label. For ordinary shelled nuts, a conservative room-temperature quality window is about 2–4 weeks; refrigeration preserves quality longer. Prefer small packs without a refrigerator. |
| Dried fruit and dehydrated vegetables | Dried fruit: about 6 months for best quality in a cool, dry pantry. Commercial dehydrated vegetables and special sealed packs follow their own labels; this is not a safety expiration. | Ordinary dried fruit: about 1 month for pantry quality; reseal and obey any refrigeration instruction. Rehydrated dishes should be eaten at the current meal or chilled. |
| Canned soups and complete meals | Low-acid commercial canned meals often use a 2–5-year best-quality window; acidic tomato products can be shorter. The exact product label governs rotation. | Refrigerate unused soup or meal promptly. Without refrigeration, discard unused contents after 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F/32°C; open only enough for the current meal. |
| Seasonings and small condiment portions | Pantry quality examples: dried herbs about 1–2 years; bouillon cubes/granules about 1 year. Sauces and single-use packets follow their own label dates; these are not safety expirations. | Keep dry seasonings dry. Follow sauce refrigeration directions; single-use packets avoid an opened bottle that needs chilling. |
| Tea, coffee and other comfort drinks | Instant coffee: about 1 year; tea bags: about 18–36 months, as pantry quality examples. Cocoa/milk-containing mixes follow their own dates. These are not safety expirations. | Instant coffee: about 2–3 months for quality when tightly resealed. Keep other dry mixes and scoops dry and follow their labels. Prepared milk-containing drinks are perishable; make only what will be consumed. |

These are ordinary pantry examples, not promises for every specially packaged emergency ration. Do not use appearance or taste to clear temperature abuse or contamination. For refrigerated food after power loss, use a thermometer and the official food-specific outage chart. The two-hour perishable-food limit and four-hour closed-refrigerator estimate describe different situations.

These estimates vary by source and storage conditions. Primed or pelleted seeds may last only one growing season. Use packet-specific germination time and a representative test before relying on a reserve. A packed-for year is not a microbial-safety guarantee. Never use treated planting seed as food or use recalled seed for sprouts/microgreens.

## Medicines, formula and pets

Medicines follow their exact expiry and storage label; do not invent a universal room-temperature tolerance or use leftover antibiotics for a new illness. Infant formula is a strict use-by exception to ordinary food-quality dating. Prepared formula has its own short time limits; see the bundled infant guide for feeding and higher-risk preparation precautions.

Pet food follows its actual label, formulation and opening instructions. Keep original lot/date information. Promptly refrigerate or discard unused opened wet food; choose meal-sized portions if refrigeration is unavailable. No universal years-to-expiry or generic veterinary medicine dose is provided.
