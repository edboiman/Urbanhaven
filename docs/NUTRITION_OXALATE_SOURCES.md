# Nutrition and oxalate source record

Reviewed 2026-11-01 America/Detroit. These are general references, not a prescribed kidney-stone diet.

## Harvard oxalate subset

Source: Harvard Nutrition Questionnaire Service Center, November 2023 Oxalate Table, linked from https://hsph.harvard.edu/department/nutrition/nutrition-questionnaire-service-center/ . Download: https://hsph.harvard.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/OXALATE-TABLE-1.xlsx .

Primary spreadsheet SHA-256: `0b20e2de100908209f68d4d4f5e337349056fae722278a9f889b2511f0b2e3aa`.

Twenty-one entries are taken from columns C (food), E (portion), G (value) and H (direct-measurement flag). Original source row numbers are retained in the bundled JSON. The numeric column does not print its unit; this interface uses the conventional milligrams-per-listed-serving interpretation, disclosing that limitation in the source panel. Broccoli and tomato paste are flagged by the source as estimates rather than directly measured values. Source wording is lightly reformatted for readability; preparation and portion are retained.

No older Harvard edition or conflicting qualitative list is blended into this subset. Food/brand and preparation differences matter. Boiled/drained entries are not values for canned versions or recipes retaining all liquid. Numeric comparison across different serving sizes is not a per-weight ranking. Ingredient links identify comparison foods; unknown ingredients do not become zero and complete-recipe totals are not calculated. Raw versus cooked, dry versus canned, and split peas versus green peas are not assumed equivalent.

## Daily nutrition

- FDA label explanation: https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-facts-label/how-understand-and-use-nutrition-facts-label — 2,000 calories is general advice; individual needs and actual portions differ.
- FDA Daily Value reference: https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-facts-label/daily-value-nutrition-and-supplement-facts-labels — protein 50 g, fiber 28 g, calcium 1,300 mg, vitamin D 20 mcg, potassium 4,700 mg and sodium 2,300 mg. The app labels these as comparison Daily Values, not universal individual requirements; sodium is a limit rather than a goal.
- USDA personal DRI calculator: https://www.nal.usda.gov/human-nutrition-and-food-safety/dri-calculator — linked for an online personal estimate. The local app does not reproduce the calculator or claim its single household average prescribes every household member's diet.
- NIDDK kidney-stone nutrition: https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/urologic-diseases/kidney-stones/eating-diet-nutrition — stone-type-specific guidance, adequate calcium from foods, sodium and protein considerations. Fluid/potassium/protein restrictions require individual instructions; no universal low-oxalate or fluid target is added.

The calorie input accepts the app's existing 500–20,000 validation range. This is a software limit, not a recommended intake range. Food-group suggestions are editorial planning aids; recipes and pantry calories do not demonstrate a nutritionally complete diet.
